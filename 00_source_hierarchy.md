# Source Hierarchy

## Tier 1 — Primary and authoritative

Original Greek, Latin, Arabic, and medieval Iberian texts; critical editions and manuscript facsimiles; PARES; BNE / Biblioteca Digital Hispánica; Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes; BOE historical legal library; CSIC repositories; university manuscript collections; Perseus / Scaife Viewer; Loeb Classical Library (where accessible); Monumenta Germaniae Historica and equivalents; CORDE, CDH, Nuevo Tesoro Lexicográfico; museum catalogues; published excavation reports; peer-reviewed archaeozoology/genetics/veterinary research; official FCI and RSCE standards; official legislation and court judgments.

## Tier 2 — Strong secondary scholarship

Peer-reviewed journal articles; academic monographs; doctoral theses from recognized universities (e.g. Laure Gisie's 2024 thesis *El estatuto jurídico del galgo en la caza* — used as a lead, independently checked, not taken on authority); critical historical/philological studies; museum and university publications; scholarly histories of hunting, animals, and medieval law.

## Tier 3 — Institutional and specialist sources

Federación Española de Galgos; veterinary associations; registered breed clubs; established animal-welfare organizations; cultural/historical foundations. **Treated as evidence of their own position and records — not as neutral historical authority.**

## Tier 4 — Discovery only

Newspapers, general websites, breed blogs, Wikipedia, commercial dog sites, unsourced popular histories. May surface leads; never sole support for a historical claim.

## Application rule

A claim resting only on Tier 4 sourcing is labeled "traditional claim" or "unsupported" regardless of how often it is repeated, unless independently corroborated at Tier 1–2.

- What tier is a given source type in this project?
- Why is a breed club's own history not treated as neutral historical evidence?
