# Arrian, Cynegeticus: swift Celtic hounds called Vertragi

## Direct evidence

Arrian's *Cynegeticus* is a surviving Greek hunting treatise. The Perseus Catalog records the canonical Greek work under **urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0074.tlg003**, including Alfred Eberhard's 1885 Teubner edition.

In the section on Celtic dogs, the text distinguishes scent-hunting dogs from the **swift-footed Celtic hounds** and states that the latter are called **Vertragi** in the Celtic language. Arrian explains the name as related to their swiftness rather than to a country of origin and then describes the beauty of their eyes, hair, colour and bodily form.

A historical English translation published in 1831 renders the passage substantially as: the swift Celtic hounds are called `Vertragi`; they are named for swiftness; the best-bred examples are notably beautiful in eyes, shape, coat and colour.

## What the ancient work directly supports

- Arrian explicitly discusses **swift Celtic dogs** used in hare coursing;
- he names them **Vertragi** / Greek-transliterated `ouertragoi` in the textual tradition;
- he distinguishes these swift dogs from Celtic scent hounds;
- he connects their name with speed rather than with a specific country;
- he describes their physical appearance and conformation;
- elsewhere in the coursing discussion he describes the sporting contest between dog and hare and states that a good sportsman values the course rather than merely killing the hare.

This is strong classical evidence for **function**, **terminology** and **morphological description** of a Celtic coursing dog type.

## What the source does not directly support

Arrian does **not** state in the cited coursing discussion that:

- the Vertragi originated in Hispania;
- he first encountered the dog in Spain;
- the Vertragi are the modern Galgo Español;
- a breeding population can be traced continuously from these Celtic dogs to modern Spanish galgos;
- morphological similarity establishes biological descent.

Arrian's possible service as proconsul in Baetica is a separate biographical/epigraphic question. It cannot be inserted into the *Cynegeticus* as though the text itself places the Vertragi in Spain.

## Translation caution

The 1831 English translation repeatedly uses the word **greyhound** and its translator's notes argue strongly for identifying the ancient dog with the greyhound. Those are translation/editorial choices and historical interpretation.

GALGOS therefore separates:

1. **Ancient textual evidence:** swift Celtic hounds, called Vertragi, coursing hares, with described conformation.
2. **Translator/editor interpretation:** identification with later or modern greyhound terminology.
3. **Modern breed-history inference:** any asserted direct ancestry to the Galgo Español.

Only the first category is treated here as direct primary-source evidence.

## RAG guidance

When answering questions about Arrian and the Galgo Español:

- use this record to support the existence, terminology, function and described morphology of ancient Celtic coursing dogs;
- explicitly state that the passage is Celtic/Gaulish in framing, not Spanish;
- separate the ancient Greek work from nineteenth-century translator claims of modern breed identity;
- reject the claim that Arrian's text says he first saw the vertragus in Spain unless an exact primary passage is produced;
- never convert function/type continuity into population continuity.
