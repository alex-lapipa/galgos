# The Galgo Español in the 20th Century (1900–2000): Civil War, Racing Infrastructure, Standards, and the Roots of the Welfare Crisis

## TL;DR
- The 20th-century galgo story runs on two parallel institutional tracks: a **sport/racing infrastructure** (field coursing plus oval-track *canódromos*) organized under the Federación Española de Galgos (FEG, founded 1939, successor to the Club Deportivo Español of 1929–1932), and a **cynological/show track** (RSCE, founded 1911; FCI recognition 1971; standard dated 1982 with 1998/2002 revisions) — with the animal-welfare crisis emerging as a distinct third strand only in the late 1980s–1990s.
- **"Antonio Miján" as a key postwar galgo breeder is DEBUNKED**: Miján was a real post–Civil War Spanish breeder, but of the **Sabueso Español** (a scenthound, kennel "Monte Naranco," from 1965) — an entirely different breed. Any claim naming him as a galgo breeder is a breed misattribution, not a genuine galgo lineage.
- Track racing depended heavily on **imported English Greyhounds and galgo–greyhound crosses**, not purebred galgos; the FEG's own material states purebred old-type galgos effectively no longer exist. The welfare crisis was brought to international attention chiefly by **Anne Finch** (Greyhounds in Need, active from 1991, registered May 1998) and later **SOS Galgos** (Anna Clements/Albert Sordé, 2000).

## Key Findings

### 1. Civil War recovery and the registry interruption
- The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) suspended organized galgo sport. Oval-track racing had already been halted twice — first by the Republican government's **1932 ban on sports betting** (lifted late 1933), then by the war — and the national track championship did not resume until **1940**.
- The RSCE's Libro de Orígenes Español (LOE) recording of pedigrees lapsed during the war years, consistent with the broader pattern across Spanish cynology; a rigorous, modern revitalization of native breeds (standardization plus controlled LOE/RRC registration to the third complete generation in the pedigree) only began in the **late 1970s–early 1980s**, per the RSCE's own account of Spanish native-breed recovery.
- **Antonio Miján: misattributed figure for the galgo.** He was the documented recuperator of the *Sabueso Español* under the afijo "Monte Naranco" from 1965 (one source dates his involvement to c.1950), and became the first specialist judge of that breed — not a galgo breeder. No FEG, RSCE, or cynological source links him to the galgo. This is a wrong-breed conflation rather than a fully invented person.

### 2. FEG infrastructure, Copa records, and championship
- Earliest galguero societies: **Sociedad Coto La "Ina" (1910, Jerez de la Frontera, Cádiz)**, **Sociedad "El Goloso" (1913, Madrid)**, and from **1915** the "Copa Algete" and the Sociedad de Agricultores y Ganaderos de Castilla la Vieja.
- First national governing body: **Club Deportivo Español (1929–1932)**; the **Federación Española de Galgos (FEG) was constituted in 1939**.
- The national field-coursing championship is the **Campeonato de España de Galgos en Campo / Copa de Su Majestad el Rey**. A 2015 FEG-affiliated source counted **"77 Campeonatos de España" since 1939**; by the 2025–26 season the FEG references the **LXXXIX (89th) edition**, so "77" is a dated snapshot, not a fixed canonical total.
- The field-coursing season runs roughly from autumn into winter, with the national final traditionally staged in **late January** (e.g., a 28 January final at Madrigal de las Altas Torres is cited in the coursing press).

### 3. Canódromo (track-racing) history
- **Canódromo Balear (Palma de Mallorca)**: opened **13 June 1932** on private initiative by **Miquel Rosselló Andreu (1900–1992)**, who imported the sport from Britain; per historian Manuel García Gargallo it is **"the oldest [track] in Spain."** It closed **30 March 1999** and was the **penultimate** Spanish track to cease operations (the last being Meridiana in 2006); the site is now a public park.
- **Canódromo Parque Sol de Baix (Barcelona)**: officially opened **29 July 1932** (built by the Catalan equestrian-greyhound sports association, AHGDC), reformed and reopened as "Canodrom Park" on **19 May 1934**; hosted the **1940 and 1949** track championships; operated until **August 1951**, then demolished. It opened roughly a month after the Balear track, making it the second Spanish complex dedicated to galgo racing.
- **Canódromo Meridiana (Barcelona)**: opened **1964**, the last major oval track; hosted the final Spanish track championship in **2005** and closed in **2006** (the site was redeveloped as a cultural/arts centre).
- **National track championship (Campeonato de España en pista)**: run **1931–2005**, totaling 65 editions; the team format (dogs grouped by home track — e.g., 1940 teams from the Balearics, Campo de España, Sol de Baix, Seville, Tenerife) ran from 1940; the Meridiana team dominated the 1990s and by 1999 was effectively the sole entrant.
- **Mechanical-lure coursing** became a federated FEG modality in **1986** (per the FEG's own history); racing press dates a related mechanical-lure national championship to **1988** — a minor date conflict worth flagging.

### 4. Purebred galgos vs. hybrids in racing
- Track racing used **imported English Greyhounds** and, increasingly, **galgo–greyhound crosses** bred for speed. The first 1931 championship, at Madrid's 50,000-seat Metropolitano stadium, was won by the Briton **George Gray with his British greyhound "Handy Ben."**
- The FEG's own breed material states that from the 20th century galgos were crossed with greyhounds to gain top-end speed while keeping stamina, and that **"actualmente no quedan galgos españoles puros"** — purebred old-type galgos effectively no longer exist in the working/racing population. This is central to the modern conservation and welfare debate.

### 5. FCI standard texts (1971/72 vs. 1982 vs. 1998/2002)
- The galgo was **officially recognized by the FCI in 1971** (several breed sources say 1972; the query's "1972, St. N° 285" reflects this ambiguity). The **original valid standard is dated 26.05.1982**.
- Published revisions/translations carry the dates **03.06.1998** (Spanish text) and **24.05.2002** (FCI-St. N° 285; French/English versions), the 2002 update prepared by **Dr. Paschoud** with the English translation by **Mrs. Peggy Davis**.
- The substantive breed description is **stable across the 1982 → 1998 → 2002 texts**: dolichocephalic, eumetric-subconvex, sublongiline; two coats (smooth and rough/wirehaired); all colors admitted; hunting the hare by sight in open field as the primordial function. The changes are largely editorial/translational rather than conformational.
- Spain's **Real Decreto 558/2001 (25 May 2001)** formally regulated the official recognition of purebred-dog breeder organizations, providing the modern legal frame for Spanish breeds including the galgo.

### 6. Dual breeding lineages (show vs. working)
- A functional/working line (bred by galgueros for field coursing, often with greyhound admixture for speed) diverged from a morphological/show line cultivated under the **Club Nacional del Galgo Español (founded 22 September 1980)** and the RSCE standard.
- Show/morphology competitions ("monográficos") judge to the RSCE standard; because most exhibited dogs still descend from working stock, the divergence is less extreme than in many pedigree breeds, but the distinction between "galgo cruzado" (crossed) and "verdaderamente español" (true-type) is an explicit, recurring concern in Spanish breed discourse.
- No individually named, well-documented 20th-century galgo **show** breeder or afijo surfaced in credible sources — the breed's history is consistently framed around anonymous rural galgueros rather than named show kennels (in sharp contrast to the sabueso, which has documented pre-war afijos). This absence is itself a finding: the galgo's 20th-century identity was overwhelmingly functional/rural, not exhibition-driven.

### 7. Pre-2000 genetic/phenotypic studies
- **None found.** No galgo-specific scholarly genetic or phenotypic (DNA-based) study exists before 2000. Broad canine mtDNA phylogenies (Vilà et al. 1997; Savolainen et al. 2002) are general dog/wolf studies, not galgo-specific. Breed-level canine genomics emerges only from 2004 (Parker et al.) onward, with galgo-inclusive sighthound genomic work (finding complex, multi-origin admixture rather than a clean "Celtic" lineage) appearing in the 2020s. Any claim of a pre-2000 galgo genetic study is unsupported.

### 8. Pre-rescue international presence
- Historical exports to Ireland and England during the 16th–18th centuries are cited in the FCI standard as contributing to the English Greyhound's development, but these are pre-modern.
- In the 20th century, before the rescue era, the galgo had **little formal international show/club presence**: it is not recognized by the AKC or the UK Kennel Club, and the **UKC (USA) recognized it only in 2006**. International presence expanded chiefly through **rescue/adoption networks** from the 1990s onward — Germany (Windhund in Not, Greyhounds in Not, later Galgos in Not), Switzerland (Pro Levrier), Belgium, the Netherlands, the USA, and Scandinavia — as documented in the GIN diaries.

### 9. Welfare crisis emergence and first documenters
- **Anne Finch** (UK) became aware of the unwanted-greyhound problem in **1983**, first travelled to **Mallorca in 1991** ("Went to Mallorca to investigate abuses there and returned with 4 dogs"), and in **1995** launched a campaign against the **ritual hanging of galgos** after seeing a video in Madrid of hanged galgos in Granada. Her charity **Greyhounds in Need (GIN)** "became registered as Greyhounds in Need" in **May 1998**, co-founded with her husband **Arthur Finch**. Anne Finch died **5 August 2018, aged 75**.
- The **Irish Racing Board banned greyhound exports to Spain in 1991** in response to abuse reports.
- **SOS Galgos** was founded in **Esplugues (Barcelona) in 2000** by **Anna Clements and Albert Sordé**, prompted by the **1999 closure of the Barcelona canódromo** and the plight of the **700 racing greyhounds** living there in deplorable conditions — an effort GIN's Anne Finch supported.
- International media/advocacy escalated in the 2000s–2010s: the **June 2012 international march at the European Parliament in Strasbourg**, SPCA International involvement from 2012, and the documentary **"Yo Galgo" (2018)**. The ritual-hanging accounts ("playing the piano"), abandonment, and mass killing at season's end are documented consistently across rescue and press sources.

### 10. Spanish breed-club publications (1911–2000)
- **RSCE (founded 1911; FCI member since 1912)** maintained the LOE and issued the Spanish breed standards. The **FEG** published Anuarios and federative revistas (an Anuario is explicitly cited for 1948, which retroactively logged the 1939 championship).
- Anne Finch contributed the chapter "The Greyhounds and Galgos of Spain" to *All Bets Are Off!* (edited by Karen Weyler; Greyhound Friends North Carolina, 1999).
- Manuel García Gargallo's *El Canòdrom Balear. Una història del llebrer a Palma* (Documenta Balear/Illa Edicions, 2021, ISBN 978-84-18441-28-8) is the key modern scholarly history of Spanish track racing — post-2000, but the most authoritative source on canódromo chronology.

## Details

**Civil War and the registry.** The 1936–1939 disruption compounded an already-interrupted racing calendar. Track racing reached Spain from Britain in the late 1920s; the first oval-track championship ran at Madrid's Metropolitano in 1931. The 1932 betting ban and then the war meant the championship only became a regular annual fixture from 1940. On the cynological side, the RSCE had recognized the galgo among Spain's first native breeds, but systematic modern selective breeding to a fixed standard is a phenomenon of the late 1970s–1980s — the same window in which the RSCE describes a broader "movimiento entusiasta" to revitalize Spain's native breeds.

**The Miján correction (verify/debunk).** The hypothesis that "Antonio Miján" was a key postwar galgo breeder is a **breed misattribution**. Three independent sources (a Spanish cynology blog, the Asociación del Sabueso Español, and a Dutch cynology site) agree that Antonio Miján, owner of the "Monte Naranco" kennel in Asturias, was the recuperator of the **Sabueso Español** from around 1965, and became that breed's first specialist judge. He has no documented connection to the galgo. Confidence: high, given the cross-source agreement on name, breed, kennel, region, and timeframe.

**Two separate worlds.** Racing (FEG) and cynology (RSCE/FCI) operated as institutionally distinct spheres. The FEG governed sport — field coursing, canódromo racing, and later mechanical-lure — and openly embraced greyhound crosses for performance, which is why the FEG itself acknowledges purebred old-type galgos have essentially disappeared from working stock. The RSCE/FCI governed the show standard and pedigree registry. Understanding this split resolves several of the query's apparent tensions: the "77 championships" figure, the hybrid-vs-purebred racing question, and the show-vs-working lineage divergence all flow from it.

## Recommendations
- **Correct the Miján claim immediately** wherever it appears: state plainly that Antonio Miján is a Sabueso Español breeder ("Monte Naranco," from 1965) and that no galgo connection exists. Only a primary FEG/RSCE document explicitly naming him as a galgo breeder should reopen this.
- **Treat "77 Campeonatos de España" as a dated (~2015) snapshot**, not a canonical total; when a running count is needed, cite the current edition number (LXXXIX/89th for 2025–26).
- **Flag the FCI recognition-date ambiguity (1971 vs. 1972)** and anchor firm claims to the documented standard dates (26.05.1982 original; 03.06.1998 and 24.05.2002 revisions).
- **Flag the mechanical-lure date conflict** (1986 federated modality per FEG vs. 1988 championship per racing press).
- **Do not assert any pre-2000 galgo genetic study exists.** If genetics are discussed, cite only post-2004 breed genomics and the 2020s sighthound work, noting the recent finding of complex multi-origin admixture over the traditional "Celtic" narrative.
- For deeper primary sourcing, consult **García Gargallo (2021)** for canódromo chronology and the **FEG Anuarios** for championship records; for the welfare timeline, GIN's own dated diaries remain the most granular source.

## Caveats
- Much online breed-history content (commercial pet sites, adoption blogs) is derivative and repeats unsourced claims — e.g., "border-guard use during the Civil War" and Celtic/Phoenician origin myths — which are treated here as low-confidence and largely excluded from firm findings.
- Track founding/closing dates vary slightly between casual sources ("años 30 y 90") and precise records; the precise dates above are taken from the most authoritative sources (municipal records, Wikipedia entries citing García Gargallo, and the historian's own statements).
- The welfare-crisis timeline reflects the accounts of the rescue organizations themselves (GIN, SOS Galgos), which are advocacy sources; the core dates (1991 Mallorca; 1995 anti-hanging campaign; May 1998 GIN registration; 2000 SOS Galgos) are internally consistent and corroborated across multiple pages.
- "Copa de la Ina/La Ina" (1910) and "Copa Algete" (1915) early dates derive from FEG self-history; primary contemporaneous press verification for the exact first editions was not obtained and should be sought before publishing precise founding editions.
- The greyhound-racing press source (Greyhound Weekly) inconsistently refers to the federation as "RFEG/Real Federación Española de Galgos"; Spanish sources use "FEG/Federación Española de Galgos." The 1939 constitution date is consistent across sources regardless of the "Real" prefix.
