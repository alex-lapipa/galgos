# Yo Galgo (2018): documentary, creator, reception and advocacy impact

## Executive summary

**Yo Galgo** is a 70-minute Spanish documentary released in **2018**, directed and written by **Yeray López Portillo**. Built around López Portillo's relationship with his galga **Bacalao**, the film investigates the world of hunting with galgos, breeding, coursing, abandonment, rescue and the wider human relationship with animals in rural Spain. Filmin lists the finished film as a 2018 Spanish documentary with a 70-minute runtime; IMDb likewise dates the release to 2018 and credits López Portillo as director and writer.

The project itself began years earlier. López Portillo has described the origin as 2013, when he was living partly in Denmark and adopted Bacalao while beginning to investigate the galgo's situation in Spain. The film then developed through roughly four years of research and filming. A 2016 Kickstarter campaign raised **€43,296 from 964 backers** against a €34,000 goal. The company **Yo Galgo Productions S.L.** was incorporated in Madrid in April 2016 with Yeray David López Portillo as sole shareholder and administrator. These dates should not be confused with the release date: **2016 is the crowdfunding/production milestone; 2018 is the completed film's release year**.

## The creator: Yeray López Portillo

A 2017 profile in *El País Semanal* describes **Yeray López** as a Madrid-born graphic designer and photographer, born in 1979, who lived part of the year in Denmark. He said the project began after adopting Bacalao and wanting to understand more deeply what happens to Spanish galgos. Later interviews describe him as a designer, photographer, traveller and documentary filmmaker whose years making *Yo Galgo* changed his relationship with animals and moved him into public animal-welfare advocacy.

López Portillo's stated method was investigative but not simply accusatory. In interviews around the Spanish release he explained that he wanted to enter a closed and mistrustful world, speak with galgueros as well as rescuers, and avoid reducing a complicated social conflict to a one-dimensional villain narrative. The film therefore combines Bacalao's personal story with hunting culture, rescue work, breeding, racing/coursing, rural life and explicit scenes of animal suffering.

## Bacalao and the narrative device

Bacalao is more than a companion animal in the film: she is the narrative question. After living with López Portillo in Denmark, she returns to Spain as the documentary asks what her life might have been in other hands. Filmin describes the film as the story of a young galga returning to Spain in an attempt to answer precisely that question.

This device makes the film historically relevant to the GALGOS timeline because it turns the modern galgo from an abstract breed or welfare statistic into an individual subject. It also reflects a broader 21st-century change in the breed's cultural meaning: the galgo increasingly appears in public discourse not only as a hunting animal but also as a companion, rescue animal and moral subject.

## Production and financing

The crowdfunding campaign ran from **27 February to 7 April 2016** and raised **€43,296 from 964 backers**, exceeding its €34,000 goal. The Kickstarter page describes the project as a documentary about the "glory and misfortunes" of the Spanish sighthound and was selected as a Kickstarter "Project We Love" in Spain.

The corporate production vehicle, **Yo Galgo Productions S.L.**, was formally incorporated in Madrid on 26 April 2016. The Spanish BORME records Yeray David López Portillo as sole shareholder and administrator, with audiovisual creation and production among the company's stated purposes.

IMDb credits **Skinny Dog Films** and **Yo Galgo Productions S.L.U.** as production companies. The finished film's original music is associated with composer **Arturo Cardelús**. The song **"Song for the Untitled"**, written by **Rickie Lee Kroell and Arturo Cardelús** and performed by Kroell, was nominated in the Original Song – Documentary category at the 2018 Hollywood Music in Media Awards.

## Release, circulation and recognition

The completed feature was released in **2018**. Filmin and IMDb both identify it as a 70-minute Spanish documentary directed by Yeray López Portillo.

The film received the **2018 Genesis Awards' Brigitte Bardot International Award for Outstanding Documentary Film**, credited to Yeray López Portillo and Skinny Dog Films. Its music also reached the Hollywood Music in Media Awards through the nomination for "Song for the Untitled."

A 2022 interview with López Portillo reports that *Yo Galgo* had been translated into **13 languages**, screened in cinemas across Spain and seen in **47 countries**. Those circulation figures are reported claims from the interview rather than independently audited audience measurements, but they provide useful evidence of the film's international reach.

## Documented advocacy impact

The clearest evidence of impact is institutional and cultural rather than a provable single-film causal effect on legislation.

On **3 February 2021**, the European Parliament's Intergroup on the Welfare and Conservation of Animals held a dedicated session on **"The Welfare of Galgos and Greyhounds."** The Intergroup's own account states that the two invited experts from Spain were **Anna Clements**, director and co-founder of SOS Galgos, and **Yeray Lopez, director of the documentary *Yo Galgo***. Members of the European Parliament and NGO representatives participated. This is direct evidence that the documentary and its director had become part of the European policy conversation around galgo welfare.

The film also entered academic discussion. A 2023 article in *Romance Quarterly*, **"Spanish Greyhounds and 'Greying' Men: Animal Advocacy and the Representation of Aging Galgueros in Spanish Documentary Film,"** analyzes *Yo Galgo* alongside Irene Blánquez's *Febrero, el miedo de los galgos*. The paper explicitly treats the documentaries as part of a wider animal-advocacy media field and examines how they represent galgueros, rural masculinity, tradition and changing Spanish attitudes toward nonhuman animals. This gives *Yo Galgo* significance beyond campaigning: it has become a scholarly source for understanding the cultural politics of the galgo.

The documentary has also remained a reference point in later campaigning. In 2026, CAS International's reporting on an EU event concerning Spanish hunting dogs recommended *Yo Galgo* as background viewing for people seeking to understand hunting with dogs in Spain. That later use demonstrates continued relevance, though it should not be interpreted as proof that the film itself caused subsequent EU policy developments.

### Impact assessment

**Supported:**
- international circulation and translation were reported by López Portillo in later interviews;
- the film won a major animal-welfare media award and received a music-industry nomination;
- López Portillo was invited as an expert to a European Parliament animal-welfare session specifically as director of *Yo Galgo*;
- the film became an object of peer-reviewed academic analysis as animal-advocacy documentary media;
- welfare organizations continued to recommend the film years after release.

**Not established by the available evidence:**
- that *Yo Galgo* by itself caused a specific law, amendment, rescue total or adoption increase;
- that reported country/language reach is equivalent to audited audience size;
- that any single numerical estimate of galgo abandonment or mortality shown or discussed around the film should be treated as settled fact without separate demographic evidence.

The historically defensible interpretation is therefore that *Yo Galgo* became an important **media and advocacy node** in the modern transformation of the galgo's public image, helping carry the welfare debate from rescue networks and rural Spain into international audiences, awards, journalism, academic analysis and a European parliamentary forum.

## Place in the GALGOS timeline

**Timeline date:** 2018  
**Evidence status:** Confirmed  
**Continuity dimension:** Cultural representation / welfare advocacy rather than biological breed continuity  
**Why it matters:** It marks the point at which a deeply researched, feature-length documentary made the contemporary galgo's contradictory status — hunting dog, discarded working animal, companion, rescue icon and cultural symbol — legible to a broad international audience.

The film sits chronologically after the 2016 Argentine ban on dog racing and before Spain's 2021–2023 legal changes around animal sentience and welfare. It should not be presented as the cause of those later legal changes; it should be presented as part of the expanding cultural and advocacy environment in which galgo welfare became a European political subject.
