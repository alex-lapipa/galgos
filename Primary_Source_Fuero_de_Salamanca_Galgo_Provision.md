# Fuero de Salamanca: source-mapped galgo provision

## Direct answer

The surviving **Fuero de Salamanca** textual tradition does contain a provision that explicitly names a **galgo** in a schedule of compensation for killing dogs. This is direct medieval lexical/legal evidence that the term was used in a normative text associated with Salamanca.

It is **not** evidence that the provision dates from the ninth century, and it is not evidence that the animal named by the medieval legal category was already the same formal breed population recognized as the modern Galgo Español.

## Reference text

The Real Academia Española's historical corpus indexes the relevant passage from the 1916 Castro/Onís reference edition as **Fuero de Salamanca §195**, under a textual date of approximately **c. 1300**.

A short transcription of the operative wording is:

> “Todo omne que matar perro o podenco de ganado o galgo, peche .ii. morauedis”

The provision then assigns a lower five-sueldo amount for a `carauo`.

### What this directly supports

- the word **galgo** occurs in the surviving medieval Salamanca fuero tradition;
- the text distinguishes a galgo within a legal valuation of dogs;
- the provision assigns economic/legal value to the loss of the named dog category;
- `galgo` therefore has secure medieval legal/lexical visibility in this witness.

### What this does not directly support

- a ninth-century date for the clause;
- an uninterrupted breeding population from antiquity to the modern Galgo Español;
- a modern kennel-club breed concept in the medieval period;
- a claim that the Salamanca provision is uniquely the earliest Iberian use of `galgo` without a wider chronological lexical search;
- a claim that every later quotation using a different article number refers to the same numbering system.

## Dating and textual transmission

The specialist **Fueros medievales** project at the University of Lausanne describes three manuscripts of the Fuero de Salamanca. It reports the oldest manuscript tradition as medieval, with manuscript A placed in the **thirteenth century** and manuscript C in the **fourteenth century** in the cited philological literature. It also distinguishes earlier layers or lost antecedent texts from the surviving manuscripts.

A BOE-hosted scholarly history of Leonese local fueros likewise states that Salamanca's **primitive fuero is lost** and that the extensive surviving fuero is a **thirteenth-century** redaction developed by the concejo.

The responsible conclusion is therefore not “the Fuero de Salamanca is from year X” as if it were one untouched document. The corpus must distinguish:

1. an earlier local legal tradition / lost primitive fuero;
2. later redactional development;
3. surviving manuscript witnesses;
4. the date and wording of the specific galgo clause as transmitted in those witnesses.

## Edition hierarchy

### Reference edition

**Américo Castro & Federico de Onís (eds.), _Fueros leoneses de Zamora, Salamanca, Ledesma y Alba de Tormes_, Centro de Estudios Históricos, Madrid, 1916, vol. I.**

The Biblioteca Virtual del Patrimonio Bibliográfico records this edition and provides a digitized object. The specialist Fueros medievales bibliography identifies Castro/Onís 1916 as the **reference edition** for Salamanca.

### Earlier edition

**Julián Sánchez Ruano, _Fuero de Salamanca_, Salamanca, 1870.**

This edition is historically important and is the source behind many modern popular quotations. However, the specialist bibliography notes that Castro/Onís criticized its manuscript basis. Numbering in later quotations should therefore not be assumed to match the 1916 reference edition.

This explains why modern secondary pages can cite the dog rule as “article 210” while the RAE historical corpus indexes the reference-edition passage as **§195**.

## Claim correction

### Claim: “The Fuero de Salamanca is a ninth-century source saying that killing a galgo incurred this fine.”

**Classification: disproven or materially misleading as worded.**

The named galgo clause is genuinely present in the surviving medieval fuero tradition, but the ninth-century date is incompatible with the manuscript/redaction history described by the specialist and BOE-hosted sources.

### Claim: “The surviving Fuero de Salamanca contains a provision explicitly naming a galgo.”

**Classification: confirmed.**

This is the narrow claim that the available source evidence directly supports.

## Related Coria / Leonese-family question

The wider family of Leonese and Coria–Cima-Coa fueros contains other dog-valuation provisions. Secondary medieval-hunting scholarship cites a **Fuero de Coria title 278** rule in which breaking a galgo's leg is compensated as if the dog had been killed. The authoritative 1949 Coria edition by José Maldonado and Emilio Sáez is independently bibliographically confirmed in the BOE/AHDE record.

That Coria clause remains **source-mapped secondary evidence in GALGOS until the exact page in the 1949 edition is directly image/text checked**. It must not be silently merged with the Salamanca §195 provision.

## RAG guidance

When answering questions about this provision:

- prefer the narrow confirmed statement that the surviving reference-edition tradition names `galgo`;
- identify the reference-edition numbering as §195 when citing Castro/Onís/RAE;
- mention that “article 210” belongs to a different edition/numbering tradition if it appears in a secondary source;
- reject the ninth-century dating;
- never turn lexical/legal continuity into population continuity or formal-breed continuity.
