# Pseudo-Oppian, Cynegetica I: an Iberian dam in dog breeding

## Direct evidence

Pseudo-Oppian's *Cynegetica* Book I lists numerous named hunting-dog groups, including an **Iberian** dog, and then gives explicit advice about pairing dog stocks.

In the breeding passage, the public-domain Mair Loeb translation renders the relevant instruction:

> put a Sarmatian sire with an Iberian dam.

The same passage recommends other pairings and then adds that, although such crosses can be made appropriately, keeping breeds/types unmixed is considered best by hunters.

## What the source directly supports

- an ancient hunting poem explicitly names an **Iberian** dog group;
- the text treats dog populations/types as distinguishable enough to discuss controlled pairings;
- it explicitly recommends pairing a **Sarmatian male with an Iberian female**;
- the wider passage discusses desired hunting-dog morphology, including a long body, long neck, long/thin straight shanks and suitability for swift pursuit of gazelle, deer and hare.

These are important pieces of evidence for ancient dog-breeding discourse and an animal category called Iberian in that discourse.

## What the source does not directly support

The text does **not** establish that:

- the Iberian dam is a Galgo Español;
- the named dog belongs to a demonstrably continuous Spanish sighthound population;
- the dog is genetically related to the modern Galgo Español;
- the recommended cross was actually practised in Roman Hispania;
- the morphology described for the ideal swift hunting dog belongs uniquely to the Iberian group rather than to the author's broader ideal type.

## Geographic caution: which Iberia?

`Iberia` in ancient Greek and Roman writing can refer to the Iberian Peninsula but can also refer to **Caucasian Iberia**. Historical commentators have therefore cautioned against automatically converting every ancient `Iberian` dog reference into `Spanish`.

GALGOS separates two propositions:

1. **Confirmed textual proposition:** Pseudo-Oppian names an Iberian dog category and recommends an Iberian dam in a breeding pairing.
2. **Geographical identification:** whether this particular reference must mean western Iberia/Hispania rather than Caucasian Iberia requires philological/contextual argument beyond the single line.

The first is primary-source evidence. The second remains qualified.

## Why this matters for Galgo history

This passage is stronger than generic claims that ancient people merely saw long-legged dogs: it shows that an ancient technical hunting text thought in terms of named dog stocks and deliberate breeding choices.

But it still does not create a Galgo Español pedigree. Even if western Iberia is accepted, the source would establish **an Iberian hunting-dog stock in ancient discourse**, not uninterrupted population continuity to a twentieth-century standardized breed.
