# Nemesianus, Cynegetica 224–230: dogs of Iberian blood

## Direct evidence

Nemesianus' late-third-century Latin *Cynegetica* tells the hunter not to rear only Spartan or Molossian dogs. It adds Britain, Pannonia, Iberian-blood stock, Libya and Tuscany to the catalogue of useful hunting dogs.

The key Latin line is:

> nec quorum proles de sanguine manat Hibero.

The public-domain Duff & Duff Loeb translation renders the sense as **do not disdain those whose progeny springs from Spanish blood**.

## What the source directly supports

- Nemesianus explicitly refers to hunting-dog progeny whose blood is described as **Hiberus / Iberian**;
- the reference occurs in a comparative catalogue of geographically named hunting stocks;
- the passage treats such stock as worthy of a hunter's attention rather than something to be despised;
- this is direct textual evidence that an Iberian-labelled canine lineage/stock existed in late Roman hunting discourse.

## What the source does not directly support

Nemesianus does **not** say that:

- this dog is called a galgo, lebrel or vertragus;
- the Iberian-blood dogs are the same population as the modern Galgo Español;
- their exact morphology is that of a modern sighthound;
- their descendants can be traced continuously through medieval and modern Spain;
- the line identifies a formal breed in the modern sense.

## Geographic and translation caution

The Loeb translation uses **Spanish blood**, and the surrounding catalogue includes Britain, Pannonia, Libya and Tuscany. That makes a western-Iberian/Hispanic reading historically plausible.

However, GALGOS preserves the Latin category first: `Hibero` / Iberian. Ancient geographical terminology and later translator choices should not be silently collapsed.

Historical discussions of classical dog breeds have also raised the broader question of whether some ancient `Iberian` dog references could mean Caucasian Iberia. Accordingly:

- the **textual reference to Iberian blood is confirmed**;
- translating it as `Spanish` is a defensible historical translation, not an independent proof of modern Spanish breed identity;
- modern Galgo ancestry remains a separate population-continuity question.

## Relationship to the other classical records

The classical corpus now contains several different kinds of evidence that must not be conflated:

- **Grattius / Martial / Arrian:** named fast-hunting `Vertraha / vertragus / Vertragi` traditions and coursing-related function.
- **Pseudo-Oppian:** an `Iberian` dog category and an Iberian dam in deliberate breeding advice.
- **Nemesianus:** hunting-dog progeny described as flowing from Iberian blood.

Together these show that ancient authors discussed both swift coursing-type dogs and Iberian-labelled canine stock. They do **not** demonstrate that the two categories were identical, nor that either is a continuous Galgo Español breeding population.

## RAG guidance

When retrieved:

- preserve the Latin `Iberian blood` concept before simplifying it to a modern nationality;
- if using `Spanish blood`, identify it as the Loeb translation's rendering;
- do not identify the dog as a galgo/vertragus unless another source establishes that connection;
- never infer uninterrupted modern Galgo Español ancestry from this line alone.
