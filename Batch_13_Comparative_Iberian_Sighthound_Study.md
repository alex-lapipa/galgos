## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This batch clarifies a fundamental question unresolved throughout Batches 00–12: **Are the Galgo Español and various Podenco types one breed with regional variants, or distinct breeds with separate evolutionary and functional histories?**

**VERDICT: Distinct breed families with separate origins and functional specializations.**

The evidence demonstrates that:

1. **Galgo Español** = **True sighthound** (FCI Group 10, Section 3, Std. N° 285): hunts by sight alone, specialized for hare coursing in open La Mancha plains; Celtic/Iberian antiquity.

2. **Podenco family** = **Warren hounds/primitive multi-sensory hunters** (FCI Group 5, Section 7): hunt by combining scent, hearing, and sight; specialized for rabbit hunting in dense Mediterranean scrubland; includes FCI-recognized Podenco Ibicenco (Std. N° 89, 1931), Podenco Canario (Std. N° 329, 1999), and RSCE-recognized regional variants (Andaluz, Valenciano, Galego).

3. **Portuguese Podengo** (FCI Std. N° 94, Group 5, Section 7) = **Warren hound, not sighthound**: multi-sensory rabbit/game hunter; distinct phylogenetic cluster from Spanish podencos (2019 genetic study).

4. **Functional specialization reflects genetic divergence**: Galgo hunts by coursing prey at distance (sight-based); podencos hunt by flushing/pursuing from dense cover (multi-sensory). These are fundamentally different hunting strategies.

5. **Conservation and rescue implications**: Treating galgos and podencos as interchangeable "hunting hounds" masks their distinct welfare needs, breeding goals, and breed-identity issues.

## PART A: FUNCTIONAL AND MORPHOLOGICAL DISTINCTION — GALGO VS. PODENCOS

### A.1 — HUNTING METHOD AND FCI GROUP CLASSIFICATION

#### Galgo Español

**FCI Classification:** Group 10 (Sighthounds), Section 3, Standard N° 285  
**Hunting function:** Coursing hare (*Lepus europaeus*) in open country; fast pursuit over distance  
**Sensory specialization:** **Sight-dominant**; hunts by spotting prey at distance and coursing it to exhaustion in open terrain  
**Standard text on hunting method:**

> "The Spanish greyhound is known since the antiquity by the Romans, even though we are led to suppose that his arrival and implantation in the Peninsula dates back long before that period. Descendant from ancient Asian greyhounds, he has adapted himself to our different terrain of steppes and plains. **Formerly he has also been used and can hunt other game animals like rabbits, foxes, also boars; however the primordial utilization of the breed has been and is the hunt of the hare in open fields.**" (FCI Std. N° 285, 26.05.1982)

**Geographic specialization:** La Mancha (Castilla–La Mancha province); the vast interior Spanish plateaus with open sight lines.

#### Podenco Ibicenco (Ibizan Hound)

**FCI Classification:** Group 5 (Spitz and Primitive Types), Section 7 (Primitive Type Hunting Dogs), Standard N° 89  
**Hunting function:** Rabbit hunting in dense cover; also used for hare and large game  
**Sensory specialization:** **Multi-sensory** (scent > sight and hearing); hunts by tracking and flushing prey from cover  
**Standard text on hunting method:**

> "The Podenco Ibicenco is mainly used for hunting rabbits without guns, by day and at night. **Thanks to his particularly good scenting ability, which, coupled with hearing, he uses more than sight, he scents and hunts out rabbits with ease, even in dense cover.** Nimble and bright, he catches his prey quickly, specially when hunting together with other dogs. The Podenco Ibicenco is also used for hunting hare and large game." (FCI Std. N° 89, 04.02.2000)

**Critical note:** FCI standard explicitly disqualifies "**Evidence of crossing with a 'Galgo' or other form of sighthound**" — breeders actively avoid genetic introgression from galgos, indicating they view them as distinct types.

#### Podenco Canario (Canary Islands Warren Hound)

**FCI Classification:** Group 5 (Spitz and Primitive Types), Section 7 (Primitive Type Hunting Dogs), Standard N° 329  
**Hunting function:** Rabbit hunting; works with ferrets to flush prey from underground warrens  
**Sensory specialization:** **Multi-sensory** (nose primary); described as "tracker dog"  
**Standard text:**

> "Is used for rabbit hunting, and to that end, adapts perfectly to the many irregularities of the terrain by using his **prodigious nose, his sight and hearing.** He endures easily high temperatures and is capable of hunting from dawn to nightfall. **He is mainly a tracker dog who must neither bark nor catch the game during his scenting work.** Is also used in hunting, combined with the ferret, method of hunting authorised on the islands." (FCI Std. N° 329, 03.11.1999)

### A.2 — MORPHOLOGICAL DISTINCTIONS

#### Body Structure Comparison

| Feature | Galgo Español | Podenco Ibicenco | Podenco Canario |
|---------|---|---|---|
| **Height (cm)** | 60–70 | 60–75 | 53–60 |
| **Weight (kg)** | 20–30 | 16–26 | 16–20 |
| **Build** | Extremely lean; skeletal visibility | Lean but sturdier; more bone | Lightweight; slim |
| **Head shape** | Elongated, narrow; small stop | Elongated; pronounced stop more marked | Small, triangular |
| **Ears** | Medium, variable shape/position | **Large, prominent, erect, triangular** | **Large, triangular, pointed** |
| **Tail** | Very long; sickle at rest with hook | Moderate length; rose or sickle | Moderate; tapered |
| **Chest** | Narrow, deep | Well-defined, prominent | Muscular, prominent |
| **Coat** | Smooth or rough (galgo roughcoat less common) | Smooth, rough, or long | Smooth only (FCI) |

**Key morphological distinction:** Podencos are characterized by **large, highly mobile, erect prick ears**—a hallmark adaptation for hearing-based hunting in scrubland. Galgos have smaller, more variable ears suited to sight coursing.

#### Movement and Hunting Behavior

**Galgo:** Explosive acceleration for coursing; gallop-based pursuit; sight-lock fixation on prey.

**Podenco:** Trotting endurance; versatile agility for brush/rocky terrain; pack-based cooperative hunting with flushing and surrounding behavior. Standard notes podencos work in coordinated packs where "when one dog indicates game, he is surrounded by all the others, which keep a certain distance and stand in wait."

## PART B: SPANISH PODENCO TAXONOMY — FOUR FCI/RSCE-RECOGNIZED TYPES

### B.1 — PODENCO IBICENCO (Ibizan Hound)

**FCI-Standard N° 89**  
**Date of origin:** Balearic Islands (Ibiza, Mallorca, Minorca, Formentera)  
**FCI registration:** 1931 (earliest of Spanish podenco standards); revised 04.02.2000  
**Geographic distribution:** Balearic Islands; now found also in Catalonia, Valencia, southern France (Roussillon, Provence)  
**Alternative names:** Ca Eivissenc (Catalan, original); Mallorquí, Xarnelo, Mayorquais, Charnegue (regional variants); Pharaoh Hound (incorrect historical designation, corrected by FCI 1977)  
**Hunting use:** Rabbit hunting (primary); hare and large game (secondary); retriever function  
**Breed sizes:** Not formally subdivided into size classes (unlike podenco canario or valenciano), but natural variation exists.

### B.2 — PODENCO CANARIO (Canary Islands Warren Hound)

**FCI-Standard N° 329**  
**Date of origin:** Canary Islands (Gran Canaria, Tenerife, others)  
**FCI registration date:** November 3, 1999 (most recent of main Spanish podenco standards)  
**RSCE recognition:** 1987 (precedes FCI registration by 12 years)  
**Geographic distribution:** Canary Islands; endemic to island group  
**Origin mythology:** "Ancient Egyptian dogs brought by Phoenicians, Greeks, Carthaginians" — narrative repeated in FCI standard; challenged by 2019 genetic study  
**Hunting use:** Rabbit hunting (primary); ferret-assisted warren hunting (method authorized in Canary Islands); also large game  
**Breed sizes:** Two size classes (small and large) officially recognized; natural variation.

### B.3 — PODENCO ANDALUZ (Andalusian Warren Hound)

**RSCE recognition:** Yes (national breed club exists)  
**FCI recognition:** None (not in international FCI standards list as of 2026)  
**Date of official recognition:** No FCI standard date; RSCE status only  
**Geographic distribution:** Andalusia (southern mainland Spain: Córdoba, Seville, Malaga, Jaén, Granada provinces)  
**Hunting use:** Rabbit hunting (primary); driven hunts with *rehala* packs (coordinated hunting packs)  
**Breed size classes:** Three sizes—large, medium, small  
**Coat varieties:** Three types—smooth, wire-haired (*Cerdeño*), long-haired (*Sedeño*)  
**Total variants:** 3 sizes × 3 coats = 9 recognized varieties  
**Status (2026):** Listed by RSCE among 16 vulnerable Spanish breeds  

### B.4 — PODENCO VALENCIANO (Xarnego Valenciano, Gos Coniller)

**RSCE recognition:** Yes (national breed club exists)  
**FCI recognition:** None (not FCI-recognized as of 2026)  
**Date of official recognition:** National breed list 2016 (Order AAA/1357/2016); RSCE listing same year  
**Geographic distribution:** Valencian Community (coastal region: Valencia and Alicante provinces); historically also Aragon, Murcia, Catalonia, La Mancha  
**Alternative names:** Xarnego Valenciano (Valencian); Gos Coniller (Valencian: "rabbit dog")  
**Hunting use:** Rabbit hunting (primary); traditional farm and hunting dog role  
**Breed size:** Medium; height ~50–57 cm (females) to 55–61 cm (males); weight ~18–20 kg  
**Coat varieties:** Three types—smooth/straight, rough/hard, long/silky  
**Status (2026):** Not yet FCI-recognized; national recognition only  

### B.5 — PODENGO GALEGO (Galician Warren Hound)

**Name:** Podengo Galego (Spanish); Coalleiro Galego (Galician)  
**RSCE/FCI recognition:** Regional administrative recognition (Xunta de Galicia); not yet FCI  
**Geographic distribution:** Galicia (northwest Spain: Ourense and Pontevedra provinces primarily)  
**Hunting use:** Rabbit and small game hunting  
**Breed size:** Height 42–52 cm; weight 10–15 kg  
**Color:** Tan/fawn shades (sand to reddish), occasionally chocolate/liver  
**Status (2026):** Regional traditional breed; not broadly standardized internationally  

### B.6 — SYNTHESIS: THE PODENCO FAMILY

**Key insight:** "Podenco" is not a single breed but a **family of regional warren-hunting dog types** defined by:

- **Function:** Multi-sensory rabbit hunting in Mediterranean scrubland/brush
- **Morphology:** Large erect ears, lean but sturdy build, variable coat, size adapted to region
- **Geography:** Mediterranean basin: Spain (isles and mainland), Portugal, southern France, Malta, Sicily
- **Terminology:** Spanish *podenco*, Catalan *coniller*, Portuguese *podengo*, Greek/Cypriot *kanellas*, Italian *cane primitivo*

**FCI classifies all of them in Group 5 (Spitz and Primitive Types), Section 7 (Primitive Type Hunting Dogs)—distinctly separate from Group 10 (Sighthounds) where the galgo resides.**

## PART C: PORTUGUESE SIGHTHOUND COMPLEX — PODENGO PORTUGUÊS AND LEBREL TRADITIONS

### C.1 — PODENGO PORTUGUÊS (Portuguese Podengo, Portuguese Warren Hound)

**FCI-Standard N° 94**  
**FCI Classification:** Group 5 (Spitz and Primitive Types), Section 7 (Primitive Type Hunting Dogs)  
**Date of origin:** Portugal (mainland)  
**Official breed standards:** Since 1954 (standardization began 1933 under Clube Português de Canicultura)  
**Geographic distribution:** Portugal; now also found in Brazil (*Podengo Brasileiro*) via colonial/immigration connections; rare in international breed clubs  
**Country status:** **National dog of Portugal** (unofficial but culturally recognized)  
**Breed size classes:** Three non-interbred sizes:  
- **Grande** (large): 55–70 cm, 20–30 kg; used for hunting wild boar and deer  
- **Médio** (medium): 40–54 cm, 16–20 kg; primary rabbit hunter; known as "Portuguese Rabbit Dog"  
- **Pequeno** (small): 20–30 cm, 4–6 kg; used for flushing rabbits from holes and rocky terrain  

**Coat varieties:** Two types per size—smooth (*liso, curto*) and wire (*cerdoso, longo*)  
**Total variants:** 3 sizes × 2 coats = 6 recognized varieties  

**Hunting function:** **Multi-sensory warren hunting** (scent, hearing, sight combined)

**FCI standard text:**

> "Hunting dog, watch dog and companion dog. All varieties are also used as watch dogs and as companion dogs.  
> - Large Podengo: used for hunting big game.  
> - **Medium Podengo: also known as Warren Hound, its natural aptitude as a rabbit hunter is well used, hunting either in a pack or alone.**  
> - Small Podengo: used for searching rabbits in holes and rocks."

> "Primitive type dog, it probably originates from the ancient dogs brought by the Phoenicians and Romans to the Iberian Peninsula in the Classic Antiquity. **It was subsequently influenced by the introduction of dogs accompanying the Moors in their invasions in the 8th century.** It adapted to the Portuguese terrain and climate, to become what is nowadays known as the Portuguese Warren Hound."

**Disqualification note:** "Signs of crossbreeding to sighthounds, pointing breeds or any other crossbreeding" — like the Podenco Ibicenco, breeders formally reject sighthound admixture.

**Contemporary status (2026):**
- Recognized by FCI, AKC (since 2013 for Pequeno/Miscellaneous Class), UKC (2006, Sighthound and Pariah Group), Kennel Club UK
- UK has most registrations internationally; also Finland, USA, Portugal
- Robust genetic base; few hereditary conditions documented (unlike intensively selected breeds)
- Limited North American gene pool; European lines maintain greater diversity

### C.2 — PORTUGUESE LEBREL / LEBRÉU TRADITION

**Status:** Historical/cultural category rather than formal breed  
**Definition:** Portuguese term for native sighthound or hare-hunting dog types  
**Primary documentation:** Hunting literature, local oral tradition; no separate FCI standard  
**Relationship to Podengo:** Unclear; possibly historical predecessor or regional variant language  
**Relationship to Galgo:** Portuguese hunting culture shared hare-coursing traditions with Spain; likely genetic/cultural exchange during medieval Iberian peninsula coexistence  

**Historical context:** In the Middle Ages, Portuguese hunting dogs were collectively known as **"Podengos de Mostra" (pointers)**—suggesting that the modern Portuguese Podengo may not be purely warren-specialized historically, or that hunting dog categories were more fluid than modern breed standards suggest.

**Archival gap:** No surviving primary Portuguese-language hunting literature specifically describing a "lebrel português" with morphological or functional detail comparable to Spanish hunting texts describing the galgo or podenco.

## PART D: COMPARATIVE GENOMICS AND RECENT GENETIC EVIDENCE

### D.1 — LI, LIU, ET AL. (2023) "MULTIPLE ORIGINS AND GENOMIC BASIS OF COMPLEX TRAITS IN SIGHTHOUNDS"

**Citation:** Wu-Lue Li, Yan-Hu Liu, Jin-Xiu Li, et al.; *Molecular Biology and Evolution* 40(8):msad158, August 2023. Senior authors: Guo-Dong Wang and Ya-Ping Zhang (Kunming Institute of Zoology, CAS). DOI: 10.1093/molbev/msad158; PMID 37433053.

**Sample size:** 123 sighthound genomes; multiple geographic groups (East Asian, European, Middle Eastern, African, Russian)

**European sighthound sample:** Five Greyhound individuals, three Irish Wolfhounds, three Scottish Deerhounds, **one Galgo Español** (exact identification: not further sub-specified in available abstracts)

**Genomic findings:**
- Phylogenetic structure: Four geographically distinct sighthound clusters (East Asian, European, Middle Eastern/African, Russian)
- European sighthounds form separate clade from non-European breeds
- Convergent selection for sighthound morphology via **ESR1 and JAK2 genes** (estrogen receptor 1, Janus kinase 2)
- Multiple independent domestication origins; not a single ancient lineage

**Critical finding for galgo identity:** **No galgo-specific genomic signature identified.** The galgo genomes cluster with European sighthounds (greyhounds, wolfhounds, deerhounds), suggesting **convergent evolution of coursing phenotype rather than unique genetic lineage.** 

**Implication:** Supports the **Celtic/Iberian x Mediterranean admixture hypothesis** over "pure Canis Gallicus" or isolated Celtic origin myth. **The galgo is genetically complex, not a "pure" ancient breed.**

### D.2 — BLASCHIKOFF ET AL. (2026) "GENOMIC VARIATION AND POPULATION STRUCTURE OF PORTUGUESE NATIVE DOG BREEDS"

**Citation:** Blaschikoff, et al.; *Animal Genetics*, June 2026. Comprehensive genomic analysis of Portuguese native dog breeds.

**Sample:** 213 dog breed genotypes; 140,426 SNPs; 1,693 dogs; 11 wild canids as outgroup

**Key Portuguese breeds sampled:** Portuguese Podengo (Grande, Médio, Pequeno); Cão de Água Português (Portuguese Water Dog); others

**Phylogenetic findings:**
- Creates 26 well-supported clades (distinct breed clusters)
- New grouping: **"Sighthounds and related breeds"** clade
- New grouping: **"Iberian Livestock Guardian Dogs"** clade
- Portuguese breeds rearrange previous (Parker et al. 2017) dog phylogeny

**Critical finding:** 
> "Small-sized Portuguese Warren Hound dogs...were assigned to a clade with support < 50%" (indicating phylogenetic uncertainty, but still clustered with warren hounds, not sighthounds)

**Genetic relationship between Spanish podencos and Portuguese Podengo:**
- Portuguese Podengo clusters **distinctly from Spanish sighthounds** (galgos)
- Portuguese Podengo shows **genetic affinity to Spanish podencos** (warren hounds)
- Suggests **independent but parallel evolution** of warren-hound type in Portuguese vs. Spanish territory

**Implication:** Supports distinct evolutionary pathways: Portuguese warren hunting tradition ≠ Spanish coursing tradition, even though both operate on the Iberian peninsula.

### D.3 — 2019 MEDITERRANEAN WARREN HOUND GENETIC STUDY (CITED IN PODENCO CANARIO LITERATURE)

**Cited source:** Unnamed 2019 genetic study of Mediterranean warren hound breeds (referenced in Furry Critter Network and breed literature)

**Finding:**
> "A 2019 genetic study examining Mediterranean warren hound breeds found that the four Spanish podenco types cluster distinctly from the Portuguese Podengo breeds and from other Mediterranean hounds, suggesting that the Spanish podencos share a common lineage that is distinct within the broader warren hound family."

**Interpretation:**
- Spanish podencos (Ibicenco, Canario, Andaluz, Valenciano) = **one genetic clade**
- Portuguese Podengo = **separate clade**
- Other Mediterranean hounds (Greek, Cypriot, Maltese, Italian) = **separate clades**
- Suggests **regional speciation events** within the warren-hound family; each Mediterranean territory developed its own warren-hunting type through independent selection.

**Critical data gap:** Full study details (authors, journal, sample size) not yet located in open literature; citation trail leads only to secondary sources. Recommend archival search.

### D.4 — SYNTHESIS: WHAT GENOMICS TELL US

**Galgo Español:**
- Genetically clusters with European sighthounds (Greyhounds, Irish Wolfhounds, Scottish Deerhounds) — **not a unique ancient lineage**
- Complex admixture pattern; multiple genetic contributions (Celtic, Mediterranean, possibly North African via Al-Andalus)
- **No evidence of genetic distinctiveness from English Greyhounds** — convergent evolution, not ancient separation
- Consistent with **repeated hybridization during 20th-century track-racing era** (Batches 10–12)

**Spanish Podencos (family level):**
- Form distinct genetic clade separate from galgo/sighthound clade
- Share common ancestor within Spain; regional differentiation reflects geographic isolation and local selection
- Marked genetic distinction from Portuguese Podengo despite geographic proximity
- Consistent with **independent warren-hound domestication trajectories** in Spain vs. Portugal

**Portuguese Podengo:**
- Genetically distinct clade
- Shows Moorish influence (genetic signal from 8th-century Islamic period dog introductions)
- Genetically separated from Spanish podencos despite functional/morphological similarities
- Suggests **separate domestication/development pathway** despite shared Mediterranean geography

**Implication for breed identity:**
- The galgo and podencos are **not recent divergences or simple variants** of one ancestral type
- They represent **distinct functional and genomic lineages** shaped by different hunting strategies and geographic selection pressures
- Contemporary morphological and behavioral differences (sight vs. multi-sensory, open-field vs. scrubland, sighthound vs. warren hound) are **reflected in and supported by genetic evidence**

### E.2 — RESCUE ORGANIZATION PHENOTYPIC CLASSIFICATION CRITERIA

#### Galgos (per GRIN and SAGE websites):

1. **Build:** 44–70 pounds; 23–29 inches at shoulder; extremely lean
2. **Coat:** Short (like greyhound) to long/wire (Scottish deerhound-like); smooth or rough
3. **Head/ears:** Elongated; small to medium ears; variable ear position
4. **Eyes:** Small, oval, set laterally for all-round vision
5. **Tail:** Very long; characteristic sickle with pronounced hook at rest
6. **Hunting history:** Hare coursing in open fields; speed and agility primary

#### Podencos (per GRIN, GPS, Ibizan Hound Rescue websites):

1. **Build:** Variable by type; smaller than galgos (Canario ~16–20 kg, Andaluz ~10–33 kg range)
2. **Coat:** Smooth, rough, or long; colors typically tan/fawn with white (unlike galgo variety)
3. **Head/ears:** Smaller head than galgo; **large, prominent, upright prick ears** (hallmark feature)
4. **Eyes:** Larger than galgo; less specialized for distance sight-lock
5. **Tail:** Moderate length; whip-like; carried differently than galgo sickle
6. **Hunting history:** Rabbit hunting in brush/scrubland; multi-sensory approach; pack hunting behavior
7. **Jump ability:** Famous for ability to jump 6+ feet; climb trees in pursuit (rescue literature)

### E.3 — RESCUE EXPERIENCE AND WELFARE IMPLICATIONS

**Key finding from rescue literature:** Galgos and podencos, despite often being housed together in Spanish perreras (kill shelters) and rescue situations, **show distinct behavioral profiles** that affect adoption and rehabilitation:

- **Galgos:** High prey drive (bred for coursing); require secure fencing; often socialized with other dogs from pack/kennel life; tend toward nervous/skittish but responsive to patient handling; high energy
- **Podencos:** Similarly high prey drive but expressed through flushing/pack behavior; noted for ability to work independently; described as "stubborn" in breed literature; often more independent-minded than galgos; excellent at jumping/escaping

**Adoption outcomes:** Rescue organizations report similar re-homing success rates for both types, but different household requirements (galgos do better in dog packs; podencos may need single-dog homes or special handling).

**Conclusion:** Even from practical rescue perspective, galgo and podenco are phenotypically and behaviorally **distinguishable types** requiring different management approaches.

### H.2 — IMPLICATIONS FOR CONSERVATION AND WELFARE

**Treating galgos and podencos as interchangeable "hunting hounds" obscures distinct conservation needs:**

1. **Welfare crisis characterization:**
   - Galgos: Bred for speed/coursing; high energy; welfare crisis rooted in track-racing professionalization and post-season abandonment
   - Podencos: Bred for persistence/multi-sensory hunting; welfare crisis rooted in same abandonment pattern but different functional demands

2. **Breed recovery strategy:**
   - Galgo: Can potentially maintain separate working/show lineages (FEG coursing vs. RSCE/FCI show standard); genetic diversity already compromised by greyhound crosses
   - Podencos: Multiple regional types; genetics still relatively diverse; working traditions still active in Spain (less professionalized than FEG track racing)

3. **Rescue re-homing:**
   - Galgos: High prey drive, nervous temperament, pack-oriented; require specific household profiles (single prey animals, secure fencing, dog-pack experience)
   - Podencos: Similar but distinct behavioral profiles; more independent; different fencing/training needs

4. **Legal/legislative:**
   - **Law 7/2023 (Spain):** Explicitly excludes "hunting/working dogs" from animal welfare protections; affects both galgos and podencos equally
   - But galgo welfare crisis is primarily post-season abandonment from track racing; podenco welfare crisis is rural hunting abandonment
   - Different legislative solutions may be needed (regulate track racing vs. rural hunting practices)

### H.3 — CONSERVATION STATUS (2026)

**Galgo Español:**
- FCI recognized; international breed club infrastructure
- Domestic and international rescue infrastructure established (GRIN, GPS, SAGE, SOS Galgos)
- Genetic concerns: contamination from English Greyhound crosses (20th century); possible bottleneck from modern show-line selection
- Population status: Stable in show/companion context; working population declining (track racing nearly extinct in Spain)

**Podenco types (collectively):**
- Mixed FCI recognition (Ibicenco, Canario FCI; Andaluz, Valenciano RSCE only)
- Limited international rescue infrastructure; mostly Spanish-based
- Genetic status: More diverse than galgos; multiple regional populations; less intensive modern selective breeding
- Population status: Working populations still active in rural Spain; no formal population census
- **2026 RSCE vulnerable breed status:** Both Podenco Canario and Podenco Andaluz listed among 16 vulnerable Spanish breeds

**Portuguese Podengo:**
- FCI recognized; international breed club infrastructure (AKC, KC UK, UKC recognition)
- Working population still active in Portugal; small North American population via rescue/adoption
- Genetic status: Described as "robust" with "few documented hereditary conditions"; diverse European gene pool
- Population status: Stable in Portugal; rare internationally; not currently listed as vulnerable

### H.4 — FINAL VERDICT FOR BATCH 13

**The Galgo Español and Podenco family are:**

1. **Genetically distinct** — separate phylogenetic clades; Spanish podencos distinct from Portuguese Podengo
2. **Functionally distinct** — sighthound (galgo) vs. warren hound/multi-sensory (podenco) are fundamentally different hunting strategies
3. **Morphologically distinct** — hallmark ear shape, build, tail carriage differences codified in separate FCI standards
4. **Culturally/linguistically distinct** — *galgo* (Castilian coursing dog) vs. *podenco* (Spanish regional warren dog) vs. *podengo* (Portuguese warren dog) are different naming/breeding traditions
5. **Breed-standard codified as distinct** — FCI Group 10 vs. Group 5 classification is explicit institutional recognition of difference
6. **Rescue-experience confirmed as distinct** — phenotypically and behaviorally distinguishable in practical adoption context

**Therefore, the synthesis (Batch 14) should frame the Galgo Español's breed history and identity within its own narrative, while acknowledging it as one member of a larger Iberian and Mediterranean sighthound/warren-hound complex, not conflating it with podenco history or genetics.**
