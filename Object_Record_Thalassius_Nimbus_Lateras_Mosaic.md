# Thalassius, Nimbus and Lateras hare-hunt mosaic, Corduba

## Excavation publication

The mosaic was documented during the 1993 archaeological intervention in the Paseo de la Victoria area of Córdoba.

Primary publication:

Juan F. Murillo Redondo, José Ramón Carrillo Díaz-Pinés & Dolores Ruiz Lara, "Intervención arqueológica en el Paseo de la Victoria (campaña 1993)," *Anuario Arqueológico de Andalucía 1994*, III, Junta de Andalucía, published 1999.

Direct PDF: https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/cultura/tabula/bitstream/20.500.11947/7476/1/AAA_1994_160_murilloredondo_cordoba.pdf

Relevant discussion: PDF page 13.

## Primary-source description

The excavation report identifies an opus tessellatum pavement in Space F, interpreted as a **cubiculum** of a Roman domus. The authors state that the mosaic represents a mounted man named **Thalassius**, accompanied by his **galgos Nimbus and Lateras**, in pursuit of a hare.

The report further calls this the first epigraphic mosaic found in Corduba and interprets Thalassius as the `dominus`, represented in a favored hunting activity.

The names Nimbus and Lateras therefore belong to the epigraphic/iconographic record rather than being modern labels invented by GALGOS.

## Dating

The preceding architectural phase is placed around **220 CE**. For the hunting mosaic itself, the excavators propose on stylistic grounds a date in the **second half of the 3rd century or beginning of the 4th century CE**.

GALGOS therefore preserves an approximate 250–325 CE range rather than presenting a false exact date.

## Evidence classification

### Supported directly

- A Roman domestic mosaic at Corduba depicts hare pursuit involving mounted Thalassius and dogs.
- The excavation authors describe the dogs as `galgos`.
- The dogs are explicitly named **Nimbus** and **Lateras** in the mosaic's epigraphic programme.
- The scene belongs to a Roman urban-domestic hunting context in Baetica.

### Interpretive boundary

`Galgo` is the modern Spanish descriptive term used by the excavation authors. The ancient inscriptions provide the individual names Nimbus and Lateras, not a formal modern breed label.

### Not supported

The object does not establish:

- that Nimbus or Lateras were members of the modern registered Galgo Español breed;
- a direct pedigree or uninterrupted population line to modern Spanish galgos;
- genetic continuity;
- equivalence between Roman visual type and modern formal breed identity.

## Continuity dimensions

- Function: **strong** — hare pursuit/coursing context is direct.
- Type: **moderate descriptive evidence** — the excavators use `galgos`, but this remains modern archaeological terminology.
- Population: **none**.
- Name: **strong object-level evidence** — Nimbus and Lateras are explicit names in the epigraphic programme.
- Formal breed: **none**.

## Research conclusion

The Thalassius mosaic is an unusually important Roman-Iberian record because hunting function, named dogs and archaeological context coexist in one object. It is powerful evidence for named hare-hunting dogs in Roman Corduba, but it does not supply the missing population bridge required for a modern Galgo Español ancestry claim.
