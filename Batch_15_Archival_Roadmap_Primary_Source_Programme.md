# Primary-Source Archival Roadmap for the Galgo Español

## Purpose

This document turns the existing GALGOS corpus into an explicit programme for the next stage of research: replacing repeated secondary claims with manuscript images, historical newspapers, official legislation, formal breed records, museum object records and reusable public-domain visual evidence wherever possible.

It is a research roadmap, not evidence that any historical claim is true. A repository's authority does not validate every search result returned by that repository.

## 1. PARES — Portal de Archivos Españoles

**Role:** Spanish state archival discovery and digitized-document access.

Primary portal: https://pares.cultura.gob.es/pares/es/inicio.html

PARES provides document and authority-record search across Spanish state archives. GALGOS should use it for royal-household records, hunting administration, municipal/legal records, noble archives, correspondence, licences and references to `galgo`, `galgos`, `galga`, `lebrel`, `lebreles`, `perro de caza`, `liebre`, `caza` and historical orthographic variants.

### PARES HTR

The PARES HTR search interface applies probabilistic handwritten-text recognition to selected documentary series. PARES itself warns that HTR results are probabilistic and not guaranteed to be fully reliable. Therefore:

1. HTR hits are **discovery leads**, not quotations.
2. Any material claim must be checked against the manuscript image.
3. Exact archival identifier, folio/image number and transcription confidence must be stored.
4. Uncertain paleography remains explicitly uncertain.

HTR: https://pares.cultura.gob.es/pares-htr/

## 2. Biblioteca Nacional de España — BNE Digital

Primary portal: https://www.bne.es/es/catalogos/biblioteca-digital-hispanica

In 2025 the BNE consolidated the historical holdings formerly exposed through Biblioteca Digital Hispánica into **BNE Digital**. The collection includes digitized printed books, manuscripts, drawings, posters, engravings, photographs, maps, scores and other materials. BNE states that its digital historical holdings include more than 35 million digitized pages and that public-domain titles may be freely downloaded.

### Priority searches

- `galgo`, `galga`, `galgos`, `galgas`
- `lebrel`, `lebreles`
- `caza de liebres`, `correr liebres`, `curso de galgos`
- `canódromo`, `carreras de galgos`, `sociedad galguera`
- named breeders, federations and clubs already present in Batches 09–14
- Arcipreste de Hita and textual witnesses where editions can be compared
- historic hunting treatises and veterinary manuals.

OCR-derived text must be checked against page images before it becomes a direct quotation.

## 3. BNE Hemeroteca Digital

Portal: https://www.bne.es/es/catalogos/hemeroteca-digital

The Hemeroteca Digital provides historical Spanish newspapers and magazines, with searchable OCR for digitized publications. It is especially important for the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, where GALGOS can move from retrospective narratives toward dated contemporary reporting.

Priority research programmes:

- earliest occurrences of organized galguero societies;
- coursing competitions and named dogs;
- British/Irish Greyhound imports and crossings;
- canódromo openings, closures and results;
- FEG/club institutional history;
- public vocabulary around hunting, sport, cruelty, rescue and adoption;
- advertisements and commercial breeding language.

Every extracted press claim should retain publication title, issue date, page number and OCR-versus-image verification status.

## 4. BOE — legislation as primary legal evidence

Portal: https://www.boe.es/

The BOE should be the canonical legal source for national statutes. For example, the consolidated text of **Ley 7/2023** explicitly excludes hunting dogs, rehalas and auxiliary hunting animals from that law's scope while stating that they remain governed by other applicable European, national and regional rules.

The corpus should distinguish:

- enacted statutory text;
- amendments and consolidated wording;
- parliamentary debate or proposed amendments;
- ministry guidance;
- partisan or NGO interpretation.

Those are related but not equivalent evidence types.

## 5. Formal breed governance — RSCE and FCI

RSCE Galgo Español: https://www.rsce.es/razas-espanolas-galgo-espanol/

FCI nomenclature: https://www.fci.be/es/nomenclature/GALGO-ESPANOL-285.html

The RSCE and FCI are primary institutional sources for modern formal breed status, standard dates and classification. They are **not neutral primary evidence for deep ancestry claims contained inside historical summaries**. The formal data should be separated from traditional breed-history narrative.

Current institutional anchors include:

- FCI breed number 285;
- definitive FCI recognition dated 10 August 1971;
- official valid standard dated 26 May 1982;
- Spanish standard publication dated 3 June 1998;
- Group 10, Sighthounds, short-haired section.

## 6. Museum and visual-object records

### Museo Nacional del Prado

The Prado's object record for Paul de Vos's *Un galgo blanco* dates the painting to 1636–1638 and places it within the Torre de la Parada animal/hunting decorative programme of the Spanish royal collection.

Object: https://www.museodelprado.es/coleccion/obra-de-arte/un-galgo-blanco/65c9e49c-0c3a-48ee-b675-02924baac33d

This is strong evidence for the **cultural representation and naming of a galgo/greyhound in a royal hunting context**. It does not by itself establish the dog's genetic relationship to the modern closed-studbook breed.

### San Baudelio de Berlanga

Twelfth-century hunting frescoes from San Baudelio contain elongated hunting dogs in pursuit scenes. Public-domain/CC0 reproductions are available through museum/open-access and Wikimedia Commons records.

These images support medieval Iberian evidence for hunting/coursing dog **function and visual type**. They do not prove formal breed identity or uninterrupted population continuity.

## 7. Visual rights and reuse register

Every image surfaced in GALGOS should record:

- object/work title;
- maker/attribution;
- date or date range;
- holding institution;
- accession/object identifier where known;
- image source URL;
- copyright/licence status;
- what the image can legitimately support;
- what it cannot legitimately support.

Prefer public-domain, CC0 or clearly licensed reproductions for the interactive site. A museum object being historically public-domain does not automatically mean every contemporary photograph of that object is unrestricted.

## 8. Priority archival questions

### P0 — myth correction

1. Locate and image-check the exact legal provisions behind the recurring Fuero de Salamanca / Fuero de Coria galgo claims.
2. Trace the earliest datable Spanish-language uses of `galgo` and `galga` in digitized witnesses.
3. Replace generic statements about early-modern exports to Britain/Ireland with dated documentary records where they exist.

### P1 — institutional history

4. Build a contemporary press timeline of galguero clubs and federation formation.
5. Document canódromo infrastructure from contemporary newspapers, municipal records and programmes.
6. Separate formal FCI/RSCE standard history from retrospective breed-origin narrative.

### P2 — social history

7. Track how the galgo moves between subsistence hunting, aristocratic hunting, sport, commercial racing, rural identity, rescue and companion-animal culture.
8. Compare vocabulary used by hunters, federations, legislators, newspapers and rescue organizations without flattening their different evidence roles.

## 9. RAG ingestion rule

For newly acquired archival records, store the smallest defensible claim unit with provenance. Search/OCR/HTR output is not automatically the authoritative text. Retrieval similarity never creates a relationship or validates a historical claim.
