# AVRA hare-hunt mosaic, Augusta Emerita

## Object and excavation context

The mosaic designated **UE 338** was excavated in the Roman domestic complex beneath/adjacent to the Asamblea de Extremadura in Mérida and published by the excavator Félix Palma García for the Consorcio de la Ciudad Monumental de Mérida.

Primary excavation publication:

Félix Palma García, "De la domus altoimperial al moderno hospital de San Juan de Dios. Intervención arqueológica realizada por la construcción del nuevo hemiciclo de la Asamblea de Extremadura en Mérida," *Mérida excavaciones arqueológicas 2002*, 8 (2005).

Direct publication: https://biblioteca.consorciomerida.org/index.php/pdf/muestraPdf/id/i%3A381603%3B/tipo/A

## Primary-source description

On publication page 20, Palma García describes the central emblem as representing a dog — in his words, **"más bien un galgo"** — hunting a hare. He also records the inscription **AVRA**, adding that it is perhaps the name of the dog.

The publication repeats the core interpretation in its historical synthesis on page 43: the central emblem shows a `galgo` hunting a hare, with the inscription AVRA perhaps naming the animal.

The excavator's terminology is a modern descriptive identification used in an archaeological publication. It is not an ancient breed registration.

## Dating

The relevant Roman domus/mosaic phase is discussed within a broad **3rd–4th century CE** horizon. Later structural phases date into the 4th–5th centuries.

Because the publication treats the building through multiple phases, GALGOS preserves the broader 3rd–4th-century range rather than inventing a single year.

## Evidence classification

### Supported directly

- A Roman-period mosaic in Augusta Emerita depicts a dog hunting a hare.
- The excavator explicitly describes the dog as `más bien un galgo`.
- The inscription `AVRA` appears beside the animal.
- The excavator considers AVRA **perhaps** the dog's name.
- Hare-hunting imagery formed part of Roman domestic material culture in Mérida.

### Uncertainty that must remain visible

`AVRA` should not be upgraded from "perhaps the dog's name" to a certain personal name without stronger epigraphic resolution.

The word `galgo` in the excavation report is a modern Spanish archaeological descriptor. It is not evidence that the Roman owner used the modern breed term.

### Not supported

This mosaic does not establish:

- a modern Galgo Español breed identity;
- direct pedigree from the depicted dog to modern galgos;
- genetic or population continuity;
- a stable breeding population identifiable across the intervening centuries.

## Provenance and conservation

The publication states that the mosaic pavement was integrated into the **Sala de los Pasos Perdidos** in the Asamblea de Extremadura as part of the conservation/integration programme for the excavated remains.

## Continuity dimensions

- Function: **strong** — a dog is shown hunting a hare.
- Type: **moderate descriptive evidence** — the excavator uses `galgo`; this remains a modern morphological/functional descriptor.
- Population: **none**.
- Name: **probable object-level animal naming** — AVRA is plausibly the dog's name, but the excavator is cautious.
- Formal breed: **none**.

## Research conclusion

AVRA is one of the strongest Roman-Iberian object records in the GALGOS corpus because it combines hare-hunting depiction, an excavator's explicit `galgo` description and an adjacent animal-name inscription. Its evidentiary value is high for function, visual type and naming culture, while remaining insufficient for modern population ancestry.
