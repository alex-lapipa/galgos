# Grattius, Cynegeticon 202–205: the swift Vertraha

## Direct evidence

Grattius' Latin hunting poem *Cynegeticon* names a **Vertraha** in its catalogue of hunting dogs. The relevant text is independently exposed by Packard Humanities Institute and The Latin Library, while LacusCurtius reproduces the Latin text, apparatus and public-domain Duff & Duff Loeb translation.

The core Latin passage at lines 202–205 is:

> Petroniost haec fama cani, volucresque Sycambros
> et pictam macula Vertraham delige …
> ocior adfectu mentis pennaque cucurrit,
> sed premit inventas, non inventura latentis.

The transmitted adjective after `Vertraham` is textually problematic. The manuscript reading and editorial proposals are recorded in the apparatus, so GALGOS does not use the uncertain colour adjective as a secure historical claim.

A conservative reading of the secure sense is: for lighter hunting, including pursuit associated with the smaller hare, Grattius recommends Petronian dogs, swift Sycambrians and the **Vertraha**; the Vertraha runs with extraordinary speed and presses hard upon game once found, but is less suited to locating hidden quarry.

## What the source directly supports

- the form **Vertraha** is explicitly attested in the Latin textual tradition of Grattius;
- the dog appears in a hunting-dog catalogue alongside other named regional/types of dogs;
- the passage emphasizes exceptional **speed**;
- the dog is described as effective in pursuit after quarry has been found;
- the surrounding lines explicitly discuss lighter hunting and the smaller hare;
- this is strong evidence for ancient terminology and a pursuit/coursing-related hunting function.

## What the source does not directly support

Grattius does **not** establish that:

- the Vertraha originated in Hispania;
- the Vertraha was a Galgo Español;
- the modern Spanish breed descends directly from the dog described in the poem;
- the population persisted uninterrupted from the Augustan period to modern Spain;
- resemblance in hunting function is equivalent to population continuity.

## Textual-critical caution

The name itself is secure enough to be significant, but the line immediately following it has an important textual issue. LacusCurtius' apparatus records the manuscript form and later editorial emendations for the adjective associated with the dog's markings/colour.

Therefore:

1. **Vertraha + speed + pursuit function** can be treated as direct textual evidence.
2. A precise coat-colour reconstruction should remain textually qualified.
3. Later etymological identification with Martial's `vertragus`, Arrian's Celtic `Vertragi`, Italian `veltro` or modern breed labels is comparative philology/history, not additional wording supplied by Grattius himself.

## Relationship to Martial and Arrian

The three source records now form a useful but carefully separated classical cluster:

- **Grattius** — `Vertraha`, extraordinary speed, pursuit after quarry is found.
- **Martial XIV.200** — `vertragus`, hare hunting for a master, carrying the hare unharmed.
- **Arrian, Cynegeticus** — swift Celtic hounds called `Vertragi`, coursing practice and conformation.

The convergence strengthens the historical case for an ancient named fast-hunting/coursing-dog tradition. It still does **not** create the missing population bridge to the modern Galgo Español.
