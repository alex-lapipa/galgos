### Batch 10: 20th Century (1900–2000) — Enriched

**Focus:** Track racing era, FEG infrastructure, breeding standardization  
**Key findings:**
- **Track racing (canódromos):** Introduced to Spain ~1928–1931 under King Alfonso XIII
- **FEG constituted:** December 8, 1939 (formal federation of coursing/racing)
- **First track championship:** 1931 (won by British dog "Handy Ben")
- **Canódromo Balear (Palma):** Opened June 13, 1932 by Miquel Rosselló Andreu; oldest Spanish track
- **Civil War registry gap:** 1936–1939 LOE registrations ceased
- **Antonio Miján DEBUNKED:** Was Sabueso Español breeder, NOT galgo breeder
- **FCI Standard:** Recognized 1971/1982 (verified text date: 26.05.1982)
- **Club Nacional del Galgo Español founded:** September 22, 1980

**Major debunkings:** Antonio Miján breed attribution (confidence: High)  
**Critical gaps:** FEG archives for pedigree verification; mechanical-lure dating conflict (1986 vs. 1988)

### Batch 11: 21st Century (2000–2026) — Enriched

**Focus:** Rescue movement, modern legislation, genomic discoveries, welfare crisis  
**Key findings:**
- **Rescue organizations (verified founding dates):**
  - GIN (UK): May 1998 (Anne Finch, died August 5, 2018)
  - SOS Galgos: 2000 (Barcelona; Anna Clements & Albert Sordé)
  - GRIN: 2006 (Abby Christman; US-based international network)
  - SAGE: 2011 (Petra Postma; Pennsylvania/California)
  - GPS: May 2014 or "since 2005" (conflict; Telma Shaw founder)
  
- **Law 7/2023 (Ley de Bienestar Animal):**
  - Published BOE: March 29, 2023
  - Effective: September 29, 2023
  - **Hunting/working dogs EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED** (agricultural classification)
  - Fines: €500–€200,000; prison up to 2 years

- **Genomics (Li, Liu, et al. 2023):**
  - 123 sighthound genomes analyzed
  - **Celtic/Canis Gallicus pure lineage: REFUTED**
  - Galgo shows complex admixture (multiple genetic flows)
  - Convergent selection via ESR1 and JAK2 genes

- **Welfare statistics (unverified estimates):**
  - Advocacy claims: 50,000–100,000 galgos/year
  - Spanish government study (2025): Only 2.8% (~425) of 15,216 shelter animals are galgos
  - **Critical methodological gap:** Killed dogs never reaching shelters structurally undercounted

### Batch 12: English Greyhound Crosses & Diaspora (1888–2026) — Enriched

**Focus:** Track racing hybrids, international breed movement, population genetics  
**Key findings:**
- **Track racing hybridization (1900–1960):**
  - Named English/Irish sire lines: Dough of Gold, Grill D'or, Finure Fergus
  - Named Spanish track operators: Miguel Rosselló Andreu, Ignacio Lucena, Manuel Mauri, Antonio Durán, Vicente Martí
  - 44 dogs exported Ireland→Spain (1912–1930)
  - 2,700 dogs exported Ireland→Spain (1978–1979)
  - **No surviving central crossbreeding studbook** — pedigrees in FEG/track archives only

- **Diaspora populations:**
  - **Argentina:** Hare introduction 1888 (Estancia "La Hansa"); dog racing banned nationally 2016
  - **Chile:** Racing since 1989, stocked from Argentina (only documented intra-diaspora movement)
  - **Portugal:** No separate galgo breed; native Podengo is distinct
  - **France:** Galgo presence primarily rescue-era (21st century)
  - **North America:** No pre-rescue galgo breeding; entirely 21st-century rescue phenomenon
  - **Colonial era:** Columbus 1493 brought galgos/lebreles; no evidence of founding self-perpetuating lineage

- **No pedigree or genomic proof** of documented English Greyhound ancestry in contemporary international galgo populations

### Batch 13: Comparative Iberian Sighthound Study (NEW) — Complete

**Focus:** Breed distinction between galgo and podenco; genomic and functional evidence  
**Key findings:**
- **VERDICT: Galgo and Podenco are DISTINCT breed families** (not variants of one breed)

- **FCI Classification:**
  - Galgo Español: **Group 10 (Sighthounds), Section 3, Standard N° 285**
  - Podenco types: **Group 5 (Spitz and Primitive), Section 7, Standards 89, 329**

- **Functional specialization:**
  - Galgo: **Sight-dominant** coursing; hare in open fields
  - Podenco: **Multi-sensory** (scent > sight + hearing); rabbit in brush/scrubland

- **Morphological hallmarks:**
  - Galgo: Small–medium variable ears; very long sickle tail with hook
  - Podenco: **Large erect prick ears** (defining trait); moderate tail

- **Spanish Podenco taxonomy:**
  - Podenco Ibicenco (FCI-89, 1931): Balearic Islands; FCI-recognized
  - Podenco Canario (FCI-329, 1999): Canary Islands; FCI-recognized
  - Podenco Andaluz (RSCE only): Andalusia; not FCI-recognized
  - Podenco Valenciano (RSCE only): Valencia region; not FCI-recognized (since 2016)

- **Portuguese Podengo (FCI-94):**
  - **Distinct genetic clade** from Spanish podencos (2019 study + Blaschikoff et al. 2026)
  - Warren hound (multi-sensory), not sighthound
  - Three non-interbred size classes (Grande, Médio, Pequeno)

- **Genomic evidence:**
  - Li et al. (2023): Galgo clusters with European sighthounds (Greyhounds, Irish Wolfhounds)
  - 2019 Mediterranean study: Spanish podencos form distinct clade from Portuguese Podengo
  - Blaschikoff et al. (2026): Portuguese Podengo genetically separated from Spanish sighthounds
  - **Implication:** Independent evolutionary pathways; regional speciation within warren-hound family

- **Breed standard disqualifications:**
  - Podenco Ibicenco: **Explicitly disqualifies sighthound crosses** — codifies breed distinction
  - Galgo: No explicit podenco mention (confirms reciprocal separation)

- **Rescue organization experience:** Phenotypically and behaviorally distinguishable in adoption programs
